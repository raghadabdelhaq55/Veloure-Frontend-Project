A day arranged
down to the minute.

From the moment a motorcar leaves our register to the moment it is collected, every hour is accounted for by a person, not a system.

The first Veloure motorcar was a 1963 Silver Cloud, kept in a Monaco garage by a man who lent it only to friends, and only after dinner. He believed a car should be introduced, not handed over — that the person driving ought to know what they were holding.

Twelve years later, that is still the whole method. We keep a small register rather than a large fleet, we photograph each motorcar the morning it leaves, and we send a specialist rather than a clerk. Nothing about the process is automated, which is why it takes twenty minutes and never longer.

“A great motorcar should be introduced, never merely handed over.”

Émile Veloure, 1963

———— The Day

Four hours that matter.

The rest of the day belongs to you.
These four are ours to arrange.

01 08:40

The motorcar arrives

A specialist brings the car to your address, washed that morning and photographed before it left the register. Nothing is delivered by a third party.

02 09:00

Twenty minutes, in person

We walk the car with you — seat, mirrors, the switches that are not obvious, the fuel it takes. Twenty minutes, and never longer. You are handed a card with one direct number on it.

03 09:20

The road is yours

No tracking calls, no check-ins. The itinerary is your business. If a road closes or plans change, the same number answers.

04 19:00

Collected where you stop

We come to wherever the day ends — the hotel, the airport, the coast road. No return counter, no queue, no inspection performed over your shoulder.
